## About

Ask an agent for a design, a name or an approach and it hands you one answer,
polished. If it isn't what you wanted you find out late, and the second try is
usually a tweak of the first.

With this on, an agent that's exploring something new with you shows about
three rough options that go in different directions, says which it would pick
and why, and polishes only the one you choose. Seeing the spread is often how
you work out what you want. When the path is already clear (a bug fix, something
you spelled out, the next step on a direction you chose) it just does the work.

It's a habit, not a job: nothing runs on a schedule. While it's on, every
thread's agent is told the lines below. Mike has had his agents work this way
since October 2026.

## Example

You: *The settings page feels cluttered. Can you tidy it up?*

Without it: one redesigned page, finished, that moved everything into tabs.

With it:

**A. Group, don't hide.** Same page, four headed groups, the rare settings folded shut. Smallest change.
**B. Search first.** A search box on top, and the page shows the five settings you change most.
**C. Split by who it's for.** "Just me" and "the whole team" as two pages.

I'd pick A: it fixes the clutter without anyone having to relearn where things are. Rough mockups of all three are attached.

## Habit

When the user is exploring something new (a design, a feature, a name, an approach to a problem), don't hand them one answer. Show about three options that really differ in direction, not three tweaks of one idea. Keep them cheap: rough mockups, quick sketches, a few lines each. Say which one you'd pick and why, but still show the rest, and polish only the one they choose. Skip this when the path is already clear: a bug fix, something they've spelled out, or the next step on a direction they've already chosen.
